# Prose review patterns

Use this catalogue to diagnose a draft. These are editorial warning signs, not
evidence that a particular author or tool produced the text.

## Content and evidence

### Portable genericity

A paragraph is too generic when its nouns can be swapped for another project,
product, organisation, or subject without changing the argument.

Replace generic claims with the actual mechanism, decision, observation,
constraint, example, result, or unresolved question. If no supporting detail
exists, shorten the claim instead of inventing specificity.

### Significance without consequence

Watch for claims that something is important, pivotal, transformative, robust,
or comprehensive without stating what changes, for whom, under what
conditions, or by how much.

### Pseudo-specificity

Exact-looking numbers, quotations, citations, anecdotes, error messages, and
personal experiences are harmful when they were not supplied or verified.
Specificity must come from evidence.

### Unsupported authority

Do not create unnamed experts, broad consensus, customer reactions, industry
standards, social proof, urgency, or attribution. Preserve uncertainty and cite
real support where the task requires it.

### Repeated conclusions

Delete conclusions that merely repeat the introduction, the preceding
paragraph, a "key takeaway", or an earlier summary. Retain a conclusion when it
performs a real task such as answering the research question, selecting an
option, or explaining consequences.

### Equal-weight coverage

Generated prose often gives every item the same space. Rank issues by impact,
confidence, urgency, or relevance when the evidence supports a ranking.

## Structure

### Canned opening

Usually remove:

- restatements of the user's request;
- broad historical or societal setup unrelated to the immediate point;
- "In today's rapidly changing..." openings;
- promises to explore, delve into, unpack, or provide a comprehensive overview.

Start with the subject, answer, observation, or decision.

### Excess scaffolding

Symptoms include many shallow headings, a list for every paragraph, bold
lead-ins on every bullet, repeated previews, and section-end summaries.

Use structure in proportion to the amount and complexity of information.

### Decorative symmetry

Review:

- repeated groups of three used for cadence rather than meaning;
- multiple paragraphs with identical shape;
- several consecutive sentences beginning the same way;
- "not only X, but Y" or "it is not X; it is Y" used as a default frame;
- consecutive paragraphs of nearly identical length.

Keep symmetry when the content genuinely has parallel parts.

### Mechanical transitions

Words such as "moreover", "furthermore", "additionally", "consequently", and
"in conclusion" are not forbidden. Remove them when paragraph order already
makes the relationship clear or when several appear close together.

## Sentence-level style

### Puffery and abstract filler

Review phrases such as:

- plays a pivotal or crucial role;
- stands as a testament;
- underscores the importance;
- marks a significant milestone;
- in the evolving landscape or realm of;
- robust, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changing, transformative;
- a myriad of, a plethora of, multifaceted, intricate.

Replace them with the action, evidence, or consequence. If there is none, cut
the sentence.

### Hedge stacks

Preserve uncertainty but avoid stacking qualifiers such as "it could perhaps
potentially be argued that". Use the narrowest accurate qualifier and explain
the source of uncertainty when useful.

### Empty throat-clearing

Review:

- it is worth noting that;
- it is important to remember that;
- at its core;
- when it comes to;
- the fact that;
- in order to;
- here is the thing;
- let us be clear.

Often the sentence is stronger when it begins after this phrase.

### Inflated verbs and nominalisations

Prefer a plain verb when it is equally accurate:

- use rather than utilise or leverage;
- help or allow rather than facilitate;
- show rather than showcase;
- is or has rather than serves as or features;
- before and after rather than prior to and subsequent to.

Do not replace established technical terminology merely because it sounds
formal.

### False universality and forced friendliness

Avoid unsupported claims about what "we all" know or what every beginner,
expert, business, or reader needs. Remove unnecessary praise, reassurance,
rhetorical questions, and invitations to "imagine" a scenario.

## Rhythm and formatting

Sentence-length variation should arise from meaning. Review passages where
every sentence or paragraph has nearly the same length, but do not force
fragments or awkward long sentences solely to create variation.

Use bold, headings, block quotes, tables, bullets, emoji, em dashes, and
parentheses for function rather than decoration. Preserve the conventions of
the requested medium.

## Final questions

Ask:

1. What new information does each paragraph contribute?
2. Which claims are supported, inferred, or still unknown?
3. Could this passage describe an unrelated subject?
4. Did the revision preserve the author's facts and legitimate voice?
5. Is any formatting compensating for weak content?
6. Can another sentence be removed without losing meaning?
